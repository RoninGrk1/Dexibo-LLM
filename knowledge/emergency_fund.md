# Emergency fund

An **emergency fund** is cash (or near-cash) set aside for unexpected costs — job loss, urgent repairs, or medical bills — so you are less likely to rely on expensive debt.

## Common educational guidelines
- Many educators discuss a buffer of roughly **3–6 months** of essential expenses (rent/mortgage, utilities, food, transport).
- Keep it in an accessible savings account rather than volatile investments.
- Replenish after you use it.

## How it relates to other goals
- Build a small starter buffer even while paying high-interest debt.
- Once basics are covered, people often discuss investing surplus for longer-term goals.
- An emergency fund is about **resilience**, not maximising return.

## Caveats
- The “right” size depends on job stability, dependents, and fixed costs.
- Holding excess cash may lose purchasing power to inflation — that is a trade-off for liquidity.

Educational only — not personal financial advice.

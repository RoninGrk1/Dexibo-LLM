# FCA basics (UK)

The **Financial Conduct Authority (FCA)** regulates financial services firms and financial markets in the United Kingdom. It aims to protect consumers, enhance market integrity, and promote competition in the interests of consumers.

## What the FCA does
- Authorises and supervises many banks, insurers, investment firms, and consumer-credit firms.
- Sets rules on conduct, disclosures, and fair treatment of customers.
- Can fine firms, restrict activities, and ban individuals from the industry.

## What it is not
- The FCA does **not** give personal investment advice to consumers.
- It does not guarantee investments or prevent every loss.
- Compensation schemes (such as FSCS) are separate arrangements with their own limits.

## Tips for learners
- Check a firm is authorised via the FCA Register before trusting it with money.
- Treat “guaranteed high returns” marketing with caution — ask for regulated disclosures.
- Educational summaries here are not a substitute for official FCA guidance.
